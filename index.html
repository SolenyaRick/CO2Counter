What this doesn't account for

This app tracks commuting, food, alcohol, flights, home electricity, clothing purchases, and (optionally, above) gas/oil heating, non-commute car driving, car ownership, pets, water usage, and banking — a real personal footprint includes a lot more, so treat every total here as a partial, illustrative estimate, not your actual footprint:

  • Non-car, non-commute travel: taxis/rideshare, buses, ferries, trains beyond your commute, cruises
  • Diet detail beyond meat/veggie/vegan: dairy, eggs, snacks, food miles/packaging
  • Purchases beyond clothing: electronics, furniture, appliances, subscriptions, streaming/data use
  • Household waste
  • Investments beyond your bank balance — stocks, funds, and pensions can carry their own financed-emissions footprint, but aren't modeled here
  • Your share of public services, infrastructure, and government spending — often a substantial slice of "full" consumption-based footprint figures, and essentially impossible to self-report

This is a large part of why official "average person" footprint figures you may see elsewhere (often 8–10 tonnes CO2e/yr for the UK) run well above the UK average shown above — that number is deliberately scoped to match only what this app measures.

"Match UK average week" sets your goal to the same commute + food UK average shown on the Stats page (only those two, since that's what this weekly goal tracks). "1.5°C 2030 (food + commute)" is our own estimate (~20.2 kg CO2e/week) — there's no official category-level split of the 1.5°C target (see the Stats page for that comprehensive figure), so this applies published 2030 reduction requirements for developed countries (nutrition −47%, mobility −72%, per Hot or Cool Institute research) to the UK-average food and commute figures above. "Match world average week" (~29.0 kg CO2e/week) is the roughest of the three — there's no global equivalent of the UK's national travel/diet surveys, so it's a rough estimate assuming less car use and less meat than the UK figures, reflecting that most of the world's population drives and eats meat less than the UK does on average.

Wasted food still carries the emissions it took to produce, so this scales up your food figures everywhere (This Week, Stats) to account for it — e.g. 10–30% waste means buying/producing about 25% more than you actually eat. Your goal colors the boxes on the Weeks page: green at or under goal, amber a bit over, red well over.
